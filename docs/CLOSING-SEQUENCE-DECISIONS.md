## 2. THE FEAR FIGURES, AND THE ONE I REFUSED

You asked for fear metrics. Every figure in the stakes section is checked against
the primary source, not a blog repeating it.

### 53% of mobile visitors leave after 3 seconds — USED
Google and SOASTA, 2017. Verified against the Think with Google PDF itself:
*"53% of mobile site visitors leave a page that takes longer than three seconds to
load."* Measured across 900,000 mobile landing pages in 126 countries.

I added the word **mobile** to the on-screen line. My first draft read "leave if it
takes over 3 seconds", which silently widened a mobile-only finding to all traffic.
That is the kind of small overstatement that loses an argument when someone checks.

### 46.1% assess credibility on visual design — USED
Stanford Web Credibility Project, Fogg et al., 2002.

My first draft read "judge you on design alone". Wrong. The study found design is
**one factor among several**, not the only one. Corrected before render.

### 75% judge your credibility by your website — REFUSED
This is on every web design blog and it is folklore. I traced it to the Stanford
study it is always attributed to. **No 75% figure exists in that research.** The
real number is the 46.1% above. One unverifiable statistic in a film built on
verified ones would undo the other thirty seconds.

## 3. ON SCARCITY, WHICH I DID NOT BUILD

Your Scene 06 brief banned it: no "LIMITED SLOTS", no "ONLY 2 CLIENTS". That ban
stands and I held it, because invented scarcity is the single fastest way to make
everything else in this film read as marketing.

What is here instead is **cost of inaction**, which is honest and does the same job:
the salesperson line, two real research figures about what a slow or unconvincing
site costs, and then your own measurement answering both. The viewer is not being
pressured by a fake deadline. They are being shown a real risk and real evidence
that you remove it.

If you want more pressure than this, the honest lever is a **dated** offer that you
will actually honour, for example a start date you can commit to this month. Tell me
the date and I will build it. I will not build a countdown that resets.

## 5. LENGTH, WHICH YOU SHOULD DECIDE ON

31.9 seconds of closing on top of roughly 90 seconds of Scenes 01 to 05 puts the
film near **two minutes**. Upwork allows far more, but attention does not.

Three ways to cut, each costing something different:

1. **Drop the 46.1% beat.** Saves 2.3s. Why it matters: the 53% figure is the
   stronger of the two and is the one your own 1.7s answers directly, so the second
   figure is reinforcement rather than argument. What happens if chosen: the stakes
   section becomes one figure, tighter, and the answer lands sooner.
2. **Drop the platform list and keep only the two lines above it.** Saves 3.5s. Why
   it matters: "your project decides the platform" still makes the expert argument
   without seven names to read. What happens if chosen: you lose the visible proof
   of range, which is the part no competitor on Upwork can copy quickly.
3. **Drop the three result figures.** Saves 6.8s and is the biggest single cut. Why
   it matters: Scenes 02 and 03 already show the Edit Lobby report and the KSUNCH
   chart in their real form, so this act is a recap. What happens if chosen: the
   closing becomes argument-only, which is weaker but much faster.

My order of preference is 1, then 2. I would not do 3.
