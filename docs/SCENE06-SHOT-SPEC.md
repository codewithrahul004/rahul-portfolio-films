## 1. WHY THERE IS NO RAHUL IN THE RENDER

You wrote "I will provide / have provided actual footage of myself." Nothing
has arrived. The only picture of you anywhere in this project is the 250px
circular avatar on your Upwork profile, which is a thumbnail, not a plate.

I am not going to generate a person, use stock footage of someone else, or
synthesise your voice for the direct-to-camera lines. Every frame of Scenes 01
to 05 is real, and this is the one shot in the whole film that has to be
unarguably you. A fabricated closing shot of the person the film spends ninety
seconds vouching for would quietly undo the other five scenes.

So the right side of the frame renders the **light** of the shot rather than
the shot: a near-black room, the monitor spilling in from frame right onto
where you sit, graded to the value your footage should land on. The typography
is being judged against the real background, not against flat black.

When your clip arrives it replaces one component and nothing else changes.

## 2. THE SHOT

One setup, one take, three times. A recent phone is more than good enough.

**Format**
- 16:9 landscape, 4K if offered, otherwise 1080p
- 30fps (24 or 60 also fine, I will conform it)
- No filters, no beauty mode, no portrait or cinematic mode. Those soften
  edges unpredictably and the artefacts show at this size
- Send the original file, uncropped

**Framing — medium close-up**
- Chest up. You should be large in frame: you are the subject now, not an
  element in a composition.
- Sit **slightly right of centre**. Your eyes land about a third of the way
  down the frame.
- Keep the **lower left quiet**. That is where RAHUL, then YOU BRING THE
  PROBLEM, then LET'S BUILD appear. Nothing bright or busy down there.
- Nothing crosses your face at any point in the scene. That is already handled
  in the layout; just do not sit hard left.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                              │
│                         ◖◗                   │
│                        (you)                 │
│                         ▓▓▓                  │
│                        ▓▓▓▓▓                 │
│   [ RAHUL ]                                  │
│   [ Web · SaaS · AI ]                        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
      type sits here        you, lit, chest up
```

**Light**
- Your monitor is the key, **off frame right or just clipping the right
  edge**. It lights the right side of your face and lets the left fall away.
  That is exactly what the scene is graded for.
- Ceiling light off. Overhead light flattens everything and is the single
  thing that will make this read as a webcam.
- No window behind you.
- If the monitor alone is too dim, a second screen or a phone on a white page
  just off frame right at face height is enough.

**Background**
- Two metres or more behind you if you can manage it. Distance gives you real
  falloff; you do not need fake blur.
- Dark and plain. Clutter is the enemy. No other people in shot at any point.

**Wardrobe**
- Plain dark top, no logo, no busy pattern.

## 3. WHAT TO SAY

You speak these lines. Your voice, recorded with the picture.

> "Hey, I'm Rahul.
>
> You bring the problem. I handle the build."
>
> *(beat)*
>
> "Let's build something great."

If the take feels more conversational, the alternative ending is "Let's talk."
Use whichever one comes out naturally. Do not try to hit it word perfect: a
real delivery with a slightly different word beats a recited one.

**Pacing.** Short. The whole thing is about seven seconds of speech inside a
nine and a half second scene. Leave a beat of silence before you start and
after you finish so I have handles.

**Sound**
- Quiet room. Fan off, AC off, windows shut.
- The phone mic at 60 to 90cm is fine. Wired earphones with a mic are better.
  Do not use Bluetooth earbuds, they compress the voice badly.
- Do not record the voice separately and try to match it later. Sync sound,
  one take.

---

## 4. WHAT TO DO IN THE TAKE

1. Start already working. Look at the screen, move the mouse. Two seconds.
2. Turn to the lens. Let the head arrive, do not snap to it.
3. Say the lines, looking down the barrel.
4. Hold for three more seconds after the last word. Settle. A small real
   half-smile somewhere beats a held grin throughout.

Fifteen seconds per take. Three takes. The third is usually the one, because
the first two get the self-consciousness out.

**Camera.** Tripod, books, or propped against the monitor stand. Steady. Lens
at eye height or very slightly above, never below. A millimetre of handheld
drift is fine and even good. Shake is not.

## 6. ONE THING WORTH DECIDING

You say "You bring the problem. I handle the build." out loud, and the same
words appear on screen underneath you at the same moment. That is deliberate
reinforcement and it can work, but literal duplication of a spoken line is
also the thing that makes a film feel like it does not trust the viewer.

Three ways to go:

1. **Keep both, as built.** The line lands twice. It is the safest for a
   viewer who watches Upwork videos muted, which is a real share of them.
   What happens: nothing to change.
2. **Drop the on-screen version, keep only LET'S BUILD.** Your face and voice
   carry the offer; the only text is the close. Cleaner, more confident, and
   it gives you more quiet frame. What happens: muted viewers get the CTA but
   not the positioning line.
3. **Keep the on-screen version, cut it from the script.** You say "Hey, I'm
   Rahul" and "Let's build something great", and the offer line appears in
   type while you simply hold the look. What happens: shortest script, least
   to perform, and the positioning still reaches muted viewers.

My preference is 3. It is the least you have to act, and the offer line reads
better as a statement on screen than as something you say about yourself.
